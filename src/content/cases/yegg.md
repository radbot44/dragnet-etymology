## 1. Prior Scholarship and the 1893–1903 Baseline

More than a century after the [*Oxford English Dictionary*](https://www.oed.com/dictionary/yegg_n) entered *yegg, n.* (1921; unrevised, opening on 23 June 1903 in the New York *Evening Post* with *"Perhaps from a proper name ... Said to be the surname of a certain American burglar and safe-breaker"*) and added [*yegg, v.*](https://www.oed.com/dictionary/yegg_v) (1993 *Additions Series*, Vol. 2; unrevised, opening in 1916 with Jack Lait), general dictionaries still treat the word's origin as unsettled. [*Merriam-Webster Online*](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/yegg) cites *The New York Times* of 15 September 1901 (`CERT-1500-114`) as its earliest quotation and remarks that nobody is quite sure where *yegg* came from.

Specialist slang lexicographers and word historians have already pushed the pre-1901 record back considerably, and each contribution warrants explicit credit:

* **1893 Disyllabic Rail-Tramp *Yeager*:** Jonathon Green's [*Green's Dictionary of Slang*](https://greensdictofslang.com/entry/3flnmoa) records *yeager* ('a tramp, spec. one who rides the rails') in the *Hartford Courant* of **23 January 1893** (p. 3, col. 2), pairing *"a moucher or yeager"* for a vagabond who neither works nor walks (`CERT-1500-101`).
* **1894 Railyard *Yegg* (v.) and *Yeggs* (n.):** On [*The Grammarphobia Blog*](https://grammarphobia.com/blog/2015/06/yegg.html) (19 June 2015; updated 12 June 2017), Patricia T. O'Conner and Stewart Kellerman reported a **14 January 1894** *Cincinnati Enquirer* feature defining *"mooch, or yegg"* as 'beg money' alongside *"T'row off de brake an' let 'er go; / We yeggs'll ride her troo"* (`CERT-1500-102`), while noting the 1900 American Bankers' Association (ABA) account of the St. Paul restitution letter signed *"JOHN YEGG & CO."* and suspect William Barrett's arrest.
* **June 1900 Safecracker *Yegg*:** On 6 October 2003, Sam Clements posted to ADS-L a **28 June 1900** *Fort Wayne News* item (*"Their lingo is the language of the 'yegg.' No other crook uses it"*), now Sense 2's opener in [*Green's Dictionary of Slang*](https://greensdictofslang.com/entry/3flnmoa) (`CERT-1500-107`).
* **1904 Reading Railroad *John Yeager* & 1909 *Century* Conjecture:** Barry A. Popik and Gerald L. Cohen (*Studies in Slang*, vol. 6, Peter Lang, 1999, pp. 21–26) examined a March 1904 press story deriving *yegg* from Reading Railroad tramp *John Yeager* (`CERT-1500-217`); Douglas Harper's [*Online Etymology Dictionary*](https://www.etymonline.com/word/yegg) summarized the October 1900 ABA report and Josiah Flynt's *The World of Graft* (1901, `CERT-1500-208`); and [*The Century Dictionary Supplement*](https://archive.org/details/centurydictionar0012benj/page/n716/mode/1up) (Vol. XII, 1909, p. 1462, `CERT-1500-225`, `CERT-1500-303`) reconstructed *"poss., through the form \*yegger, < G. jäger, hunter."*

What remained unresolved was how an 1893–1894 freight-riding beggar's term became national detective parlance for nitroglycerin safe-blowers in 1900, whether the August 1899 St. Paul theft or William Barrett involved safecracking, and whether Romani or Chinatown claims withstood primary lexicons.

## 2. Pinkerton's January–September 1900 Safecracking Dispatches

Prior accounts assumed *yegg* entered print from the Richmond ABA convention of 2–4 October 1900. Primary scans show instead that Pinkerton's National Detective Agency launched *yegg*, *yeggs*, *yeggman*, and *John yeggmen* across newsprint and trade journals nearly nine months earlier—without mentioning St. Paul or any founder named John Yegg:

* **22–23 January 1900 Associated Press Wire (`CERT-1500-103`, `CERT-1500-104`):** After Newark detectives arrested Henry Ogden ("Connecticut Blackie"), Frank Lashaway ("New York White"), and James Murphy ("Big Jim Murphy") for shooting watchman Charles Morris in Bridgeport, an AP wire in [*The Lowell Sun* (22 January 1900, p. 1, col. 5)](https://archive.org/details/lowell-sun-1900-01-22/page/n7/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-103`) and the [*Norwich Bulletin* (23 January 1900, p. 4, col. 4)](https://archive.org/details/norwichbulletin100bull_16/page/n167/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-104`) antedates *OED* (1903) by 3 years and 5 months and *Green's* June 1900 quote by 5 months:

> NEW YORK, Jan. 22.—Henry Ogden, alias "Connecticut Blackie," who has served time in Montana; Frank Lashaway, alias "New York White," who has been in the Albany penitentiary, and James Murphy, known to the rogues' gallery as "Big Jim Murphy," are under arrest at Newark, N. J. They are said to be members of what is known as the "Yegg Fraternity" of safe-crackers.

* **23 January / 14 February 1900 Jewelers' Security Alliance Report (`CERT-1500-105`):** Dated *"NEW YORK, January 23rd, 1900"*, Robert A. Pinkerton's protective report in [*The Jewelers' Circular-Weekly* (14 February 1900, Vol. 40, No. 3, p. 32d)](https://archive.org/details/sim_jck_1900-02-14_40_3/page/n34/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-105`)—masked in OCR as `"veggs"`—supplies the earliest verified safecracker plural *yeggs* and compound *yeggmen*, dating nitroglycerin yegg bands to c. 1897:

> Within the past three years a new and dangerous class of burglars, known among themselves as "yeggs" or "yeggmen," have been operating very successfully, and some of them have become such adepts in the use of nitro-glycerine, and its manufacture from giant powder, that few safes can withstand their attacks if they have an hour's time to do the work.

* **May / 3 June 1900 Cincinnati Police Chiefs' Paper (`CERT-1500-106`):** At the May 1900 Cincinnati Police Chiefs' convention, William A. Pinkerton read a paper printed in the [*St. Louis Post-Dispatch* (3 June 1900, p. 30, cols. 3–7)](https://archive.org/details/per_st-louis-post-dispatch_1900-06-03_52_286/page/n29/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-106`), 25 days before *Green's* Fort Wayne item: *'These "yeggmen," known to their associates as "John yeggmen," constitute a class of burglars which have grown up in the United States during the last ten years. These men are sometimes called "yeggs."'* Lowercase *y* in *"John yeggmen"* marks *John* as a generic personifier rather than a surname.
* **August–September 1900 National Diffusion (`CERT-1500-108`–`CERT-1500-110`, `CERT-1500-115`, `CERT-1500-201`–`CERT-1500-203`, `CERT-1500-214`, `CERT-1500-215`):** [*The New York Times* (12 August 1900, p. 12, col. 1)](https://archive.org/details/NYTimes-Aug1-Sep30-1900/page/n194/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-108`, `CERT-1500-201`) covered John Butler (*'"Frisco Slim" is known as the "King" of the "yeggmen"'*), self-antedating *Merriam-Webster*'s 15 September 1901 *New York Times* baseline (`CERT-1500-114`; cf. 13 August 1901, `CERT-1500-113`) by 13 months. South San Francisco's [*The Enterprise* (8 September 1900, p. 2)](https://archive.org/details/cssf_000277/page/n1/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-109`, `CERT-1500-202`) printed *'Johnny Yeggmen'*—24 years before *Green's* 1924 baseline—and *'the yegg'*, translated in Chicago's [*Abendpost* (25 September 1900, p. 6)](https://archive.org/details/sim_abendpost-sonntagpost_1900-09-25_12_228/page/n5/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-203`) and followed in 1901–1903 newspapers and federal escape fliers.

## 3. Verbal Conversion: Antedating *Yegg, v.* to 1905–1913

Beyond the 1894 *Cincinnati Enquirer* mendicant verb ('beg money', `CERT-1500-102`), conversion of the safecracker noun into *yegg, v.* ('to commit safe-burglary or robbery') preceded *OED*'s 1916 Jack Lait baseline by eleven years:

* **2 June 1905 (*yegging*, gerund — 11 years before *OED*):** Profiling locator Darris, the [*St. Louis Post-Dispatch* (2 June 1905, p. 4, col. 4)](https://archive.org/details/per_st-louis-post-dispatch_st-louis-post-dispatch_1905-06-02_57_285/page/n3/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-116`) notes: *"he prefers the avocation of beggar as involving less exertion and less risk than 'log rolling' or 'yegging.'"*
* **9 June 1906 (*to yegg*, transitive infinitive — 10 years before *OED*):** [*Judge* (Vol. 50, No. 1286, leaf 5)](https://archive.org/details/sim_judge_1906-06-09_50_1286/page/n4/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-117`) prints: *"Why doesn't he chloroform us if he is [going] to yegg the game from us that way?"*
* **1908–1913 Finite and Transitive Uses (`CERT-1500-118`–`CERT-1500-122`):** [*Everybody's Magazine* (June 1908, p. 726)](https://archive.org/details/sim_everybodys-magazine_1908-06_18_6/page/n35/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-118`) has *"going to yegg it"*; [*The Saturday Evening Post* (16 January 1909, p. 8)](https://archive.org/details/sim_saturday-evening-post_1909-01-16_181_29/page/n9/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-119`) prints *"yegging"*; [*Everybody's Magazine* (March 1910, p. 294)](https://archive.org/details/sim_everybodys-magazine_1910-03_22_3/page/n39/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-120`) attests *"He had once 'yegged it'"*; and the [*St. Louis Post-Dispatch* (25 September 1912, p. 9, col. 4)](https://archive.org/details/per_st-louis-post-dispatch_1912-09-25_65_36/page/n8/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-121`) prints *"as if he yegged the dough out of a strong box"*, followed in MacGrath's [*Deuces Wild* (1913, pp. 93, 113)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.168547/page/n98/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-122`).

## 4. The 1899 St. Paul Sneak Theft, William Barrett (`345S`), and "John Yegg"

Primary ABA Protective Committee scans refute deriving *yegg* from an 1899 St. Paul safe-blowing job or from William Barrett:

1. **A Noonday Window Sneak Theft (`CERT-1500-204`, `CERT-1500-205`):** In the [*Commercial and Financial Chronicle Supplement* (13 October 1900, p. 62)](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/publications/cfc/cfc_19001013_supplement.pdf) (`CERT-1500-204`), the 9 August 1899 Scandinavian-American Bank loss in St. Paul involved no safe-blowing: two sneak thieves telephoned the teller's cage occupant while fishing $540 through the window, returning it signed **`"Yours respectfully John Yegg & Co."`** ([*Los Angeles Times*, 12 July 1903, Magazine, p. 5](https://archive.org/details/sim_los-angeles-times_the-los-angeles-times_1903-07-12/page/n52/mode/1up), `CERT-1500-205`).
2. **William Barrett (`345S`), Urban Bank Sneak (`CERT-1500-206`):** The [*1905 ABA Proceedings* (pp. 35–36, 55)](https://archive.org/details/proceedingsofann31amer/page/n79/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-206`) classify **William Barrett** (Portrait `345S`, Bulletin No. 4) strictly under *"Sneak Thieves"*, segregated from *"Yegg" Burglars*. Arrested in Portland on 1 May 1905 with bank sneaks **Fritzie Dhien** and **G. H. Hanlon** after stealing $20,000 in gold in San Francisco, Barrett served 2 years and 3 months in the Maryland Penitentiary.
3. **Zero Historical *John Yegg* vs. Six Contradictory Tales:** Audits across 3.71 million characters of Flynt's [*Tramping with Tramps* (1899)](https://archive.org/details/trampingwithtra00willgoog/page/n392/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-209`), [Byrnes (1886)](https://archive.org/details/professionalcrim00byrn_0/page/n15/mode/1up), Wooldridge (1901/1906), and Hapgood (1903) (`CERT-1500-210`), plus 1870–1905 prison registers (`CERT-1500-222`), find zero convicts named *John Yegg*, whereas actual Pinkerton files name explosive pioneer **Thomas James, alias "The Rambler"** ([1906 *ABA Proceedings*, p. 51](https://archive.org/details/americanbankersa0000jame/page/n83/mode/1up), `CERT-1500-221`). After Flynt challenged detectives in [*McClure's* (February 1901, pp. 331, 333)](https://archive.org/details/sim_new-mcclures-magazine_1901-02_16_4/page/n45/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-111`, `CERT-1500-207`), sources improvised six conflicting tales: a California hobo ([*Savannah Morning News*, 16 May 1901](https://gahistoricnewspapers.galileo.usg.edu/lccn/sn89053684/1901-05-16/ed-1/seq-4/), `CERT-1500-112`, `CERT-1500-211`–`CERT-1500-213`), *"John W. Yegg"* ([*Ypsilanti Sentinel-Commercial*, 3 September 1903](https://archive.org/details/ypsilanti_sentinel-commercial_1903.09.03/page/n5/mode/1up), `CERT-1500-216`), Reading Railroad *"John Yeager"* (`CERT-1500-217`), W. A. Pinkerton's gypsy *"Yegg"* claim ([*American Lawyer*, September 1904](https://archive.org/details/sim_american-lawyer-1893_1904-09_12/page/n9/mode/1up), `CERT-1500-218`), *"John Yegg, a Swede"* ([*Everybody's*, September 1904](https://archive.org/details/sim_everybodys-magazine_1904-09_11_3/page/n137/mode/1up), `CERT-1500-220`), and a Michigan electrician (1908)—while [*The New York Times* (25 December 1904, Magazine, p. 3)](https://archive.org/details/sim_new-york-times_1904-12-25_54_17153/page/n18/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-219`) preserved **`"Beggar.—John-bum, or John-Yegg."`**

In Horne's [*Pennsylvania German Manual* (1896 [1875], pp. 199, 229)](https://archive.org/details/pennsylvaniager00horngoog/page/n208/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-301`), German *Jäger* is spelled **`yagăr, hunter, jaeger`**, and *jagen* (`yawgă`) is glossed **`dŏrchsuchă`** ('ransack'). Along Mid-Atlantic rail lines—the *Kunden* layer behind *bum* (< *Bummler*) and *bindle* (< *Bündel*), including the Reading Railroad remembered in 1904 (`CERT-1500-217`)—*yagăr* entered cant as *yeager* (1893, `CERT-1500-101`), reinforced by Scots *yagger* (`CERT-1500-307`), before `-er` reanalysis back-formed *yegg* and *yeggs* by January 1894 (`CERT-1500-102`, cf. *burglar* -> *burgle*).

Conversely, Jack Black's [*You Can't Win* (1926, p. 172)](https://archive.org/details/youcantwin0000jack/page/172/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-304`) surfaced thirty-three years after 1893 *yeager*. Williams's [*Tonic Dictionary* (1856, pp. 76, 554, 674, 695–701)](https://archive.org/details/cyingcwcfanwan00williala/page/n738/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-305`), Eitel (1877), and Ball's [*Cantonese Made Easy* (1888, p. 125)](https://archive.org/details/cantonesemadeeas00ballrich/page/n164/mode/1up) (`CERT-1500-306`) prove Cantonese uses *hat í* (`乞兒`) for 'beggar' and *ts'ák* (`賊`) for 'thief', whereas **喫飯 *yák fán*** ('eat rice!') explains the cook-oven call folk-etymologized onto *yeggman*. Finally, the ledger excludes *Scientific American*'s 27 January 1906 issue misreprinted in 2020 as "January 1856" (`CERT-1500-123`), bound-serial catalog misdates (`CERT-1500-223`, `CERT-1500-224`), and pre-1900 OCR ghosts turning *yesterday* (1891) and *begging* (1894) into `yegg` and `Yegging` (`CERT-1500-124`).
