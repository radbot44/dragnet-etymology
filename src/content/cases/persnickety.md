## What the primary scans establish

North American *persnickety* is a late nineteenth-century sibilant reshaping of Lowland Scots [*pernickety*](https://www.oed.com/dictionary/pernickety_adj) ([*pernicketie*](https://dsl.ac.uk/entry/snd/pernicketie)). While the [Oxford English Dictionary](https://www.oed.com/dictionary/persnickety_adj) dates American *persnickety* from 1885 in the Canton, Ohio *Repository* (held on offline microfilm at the [Stark County District Library](https://www.starklibrary.org/family-and-local-history/stark-county-index-files/)) and [*persnicketiness*](https://www.oed.com/dictionary/persnicketiness_n) from 1913 in *The Rock Island Argus*, systematic inspection of sixty-three pre-1931 book, dictionary, census, and periodical scans refines both ends of the transatlantic timeline without claiming a pre-1885 sibilant antedating.

### First-edition Lowland Scots lineage, 1721–1882

Modern reference summaries routinely report John Jamieson’s 1808 dictionary entry as three-syllable *pernicktie* and date four-syllable *pernicketie* to his 1825 *Supplement*. Physical inspection of [Jamieson’s 1808 *Etymological Dictionary*, Vol. II, sig. 3G2r](https://archive.org/details/etymologicaldict02jami/page/n212/mode/1up) shows that the 1808 first edition already prints four-syllable **`PERNICKITIE, adj.`** in clear roman capitals: “Precise in trifles; applied also to dress, denoting trimness, S. *perjink* synon.” (CERT-3100-304). Pre-1808 Scots glossaries preserve the morphological building blocks separately: *nicket* (“a small notch”) and *Perquire* (“by heart, exactly”) in [Allan Ramsay’s *Poems* (1721)](https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_poems-by-allan-ramsay_ramsay-allan_1721_1/page/n226/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-301), *perjink* and *perqueer* in [James Elphinston’s *Propriety Ascertained*, Vol. II (1787)](https://archive.org/details/proprietyascerta00elph/page/n583/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-302), and *Nicketts* alongside *Nackie* in [James Sibbald’s *Chronicle of Scottish Poetry*, Vol. IV (1802)](https://archive.org/details/10750174bsb/page/n220/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-303).

First-edition literary collation resolves two further nineteenth-century Scots cruxes. In [John Galt’s January 1822 first edition of *Sir Andrew Wylie*, Vol. III, p. 181](https://archive.org/details/sirandrewwyliet07galtgoog/page/n188/mode/1up), the plural noun cited from later reprints as *pernicketies* actually reads **`perknicketties`** (“Surely we’re no to stand on such perknicketties”), preserving an orthographic link to *knick* and *knick-knack* alongside Galt’s May 1822 [*The Provost*](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.549886/page/n243/mode/1up) (*pernicketty*, immediately reprinted in New York by J. & J. Harper as *per-nicketty*; CERT-3100-305, CERT-3100-306). Similarly, collating [J. A. Froude’s *Thomas Carlyle*, Vol. I (1882), p. 31](https://archive.org/details/thomascarlyle01frou/page/31/mode/1up) (*pernicked* in Carlyle’s 1814 letter) against [David Masson’s November 1881 *Macmillan’s Magazine* article, p. 72](https://archive.org/details/macmillansmagaz43grovgoog/page/n88/mode/1up) (*per-nickety*) explains why *OED1* cited 1814 for *pernickety* while *SND* cited 1814 for participial *pernicked* (CERT-3100-313). Subsequent Scots milestones run cleanly through [Jamieson’s 1825 *Supplement*](https://archive.org/details/aetymologicaldi00jamigoog/page/n223/mode/1up) (*PIRNICKERIE*, Roxburghshire *PRIGNICKITIE*; CERT-3100-307), [John Wilson’s October 1826 *Blackwood’s* dialogue](https://archive.org/details/sim_blackwoods-magazine_1826-10_20_118/page/n101/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-308), [Christian Isobel Johnstone’s 1827 *Elizabeth de Bruce*, Vol. I](https://archive.org/details/elizabethdebruce01johns/page/n45/mode/1up) (*Auld Pernickitie*, *our Pernickitie Laird*; CERT-3100-309), [James Stewart’s 1857 *Sketches*](https://archive.org/details/nd_sketchesofscottishcharacter00notre_g57/page/n213/mode/1up) (*Pirnicky*; CERT-3100-310), [George MacDonald’s 1868 *Robert Falconer*, Vol. II](https://archive.org/details/robertfalconer02macd/page/n163/mode/1up) (“pernickety (precise)”; CERT-3100-311), and [Jamieson’s 1880 revised edition](https://archive.org/details/etymologicaldict03jamiuoft/page/476/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-312).

### The North American bridge (1834–1880) and the 1885 sibilant witnesses

Non-sibilant *pernickity* and *pernicketty* were already naturalized in American print decades before 1885. Following Galt’s 1822 New York reprint, Seba Smith’s burlesque [*The Life of Andrew Jackson* (Philadelphia, 1834), pp. 66, 78](https://archive.org/details/lifeofandrewjack00smit/page/n81/mode/1up) deployed *pernickity* five times in American political satire (CERT-3100-101). After the Civil War, American periodicals both glossed Edinburgh usage ([*Putnam’s Magazine*, May 1870, pp. 541–545](https://archive.org/details/sim_putnams-magazine_1870-05_5_29/page/n28/mode/1up); [*New-York Daily Tribune*, September 15, 1870, p. 2](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030214/1870-09-15/ed-1/?sp=2); CERT-3100-102, CERT-3100-103) and used the non-sibilant form in domestic Ohio journalism, including [*The Cincinnati Daily Star* (November 10, 1879, p. 2)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85025759/1879-11-10/ed-1/?sp=2) and [*The Mahoning Dispatch* (Canfield, Ohio, September 17, 1880, p. 1)](https://ohiomemory.org/digital/collection/p16007coll62/id/1387/)—published in Mahoning County directly on Stark County’s eastern border five years before the 1885 *Canton Repository* witness (CERT-3100-104, CERT-3100-105). Western Pennsylvania still reported non-sibilant *pernickety : cranky* in [*Dialect Notes*, Vol. I, Part II (1890; errata 1892)](https://archive.org/details/dialectnotes01newhuoft/page/62/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-206).

Two independent open-scan texts from **1885** corroborate the OED’s 1885 emergence horizon:

1. **September 22, 1885 (*persnickity*)**: [*The Boston Weekly Globe*, p. 5, col. 2](https://archive.org/details/boston-weekly-globe-1885-09-22/page/n4/mode/1up), reprinting a *Chicago Herald* theatrical interview: “In New York I understand they are not quite so persnickity, but I don’t want New York in mine.” (CERT-3100-106).
2. **October 1885, composed before June 26, 1885 (*persnickerty*)**: [Metta Victoria Fuller Victor, *The Bad Boy at Home* (New York: J. S. Ogilvie & Co., copyright 1885), p. 110](https://archive.org/details/badboyathomeand00victgoog/page/n112/mode/1up), written by an author raised in Wooster, Wayne County, Ohio (immediately west of Stark County): “I had to go to being plane Georgie again, without no persnickerty clothes” (CERT-3100-107).

By 1889–1892, the sibilant form had stabilized in national magazines and newspaper syndication: Emerson Hough used *persnickity* in [*Forest and Stream* (August 1, 1889, p. 27)](https://archive.org/details/ForeststreamXXXIB/page/n36/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-108), S. Boggs printed standard **`persnickety`** in [*Good Housekeeping* (October 26, 1889, p. 298)](https://archive.org/details/sim_good-housekeeping_good-housekeeping_1889-10-26_9_13/page/n9/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-109), and coast-to-coast 1892 syndication spread the word through the [*New-York Daily Tribune* (February 21, 1892, ex *San Francisco Argonaut*)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030214/1892-02-21/ed-1/?sp=18), [*The Evening World* (April 1, 1892)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030193/1892-04-01/ed-1/?sp=5), Mansfield actor A. F. Nail’s copyright-stamped play [*The Drummer Boy* (November 23, 1892, p. 37)](https://archive.org/details/drummerboy00nail/page/n42/mode/1up), and [*Life* (February 25, 1897, p. 155)](https://archive.org/details/sim_life_1897-02-25_29_740/page/n12/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-110, CERT-3100-111, CERT-3100-112, CERT-3100-207).

### Derivatives, grammatical shifts, and reverse transatlantic export

- **Abstract nouns in *-ness* and *-ism***: Following James Brown’s Morayshire coinage *picknickerty-ism* in [*The Round Table Club* (1873), p. 279](https://archive.org/details/roundtableclubo00elgigoog/page/n290/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-202), non-sibilant **`pernicketyness`** appeared in [*The Engineer* (August 3, 1888, p. 100)](https://archive.org/details/sim_engineer_july-06-december-28-1888_66/page/100/mode/1up) and **`pernicketiness`** in the [National Educational Association *Proceedings* (1897, p. 846)](https://archive.org/details/bwb_P9-BJI-412/page/846/mode/1up) (paired with Marcus Dods’s July 1854 Edinburgh letter first printed in [November 1910, p. 62](https://archive.org/details/earlylettersofma00dodsuoft/page/62/mode/1up); CERT-3100-203, CERT-3100-204, CERT-3100-201). Scan verification of the OED’s 1913 baseline for sibilant [*persnicketiness*](https://www.oed.com/dictionary/persnicketiness_n) in [*The Rock Island Argus* (February 17, 1913, p. 6, col. 6)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn92053934/1913-02-17/ed-1/?sp=6) identifies its author as syndicated Cleveland domestic-science columnist **Mrs. Alice Gitchell Kirk**—placing the *-ness* derivative right back in Northern Ohio (CERT-3100-205).
- **Adverbial, substantive, superlative, and labialized forms**: Louise Pound’s Nebraska list in [*Dialect Notes*, Vol. III, Part I (1905), p. 63](https://archive.org/details/dialectnotes03newhuoft/page/63/mode/1up) gives the first dialect-dictionary entry for *persnickety* (“Disagreeable, or snippy”) and documents flat-adverbial syntax (“They acted mighty persnickety”), followed by general colloquial status in [*Dialect Notes*, Vol. IV, Part III (1915), p. 215](https://archive.org/details/dialectnotes04newhuoft/page/215/mode/1up), substantive plural conversion (“the persnickety themselves”) in [*The National Underwriter* (February 24, 1921, p. 8)](https://archive.org/details/sim_national-underwriter_1921-02-24_25th-year_8/page/8/mode/1up), superlative **`persnicketiest`** in Mary O’Connor Newell’s [*Chicago Daily Tribune* column (June 12, 1923, p. 19)](https://archive.org/details/per_chicago-daily-tribune_1923-06-12_82_140/page/n18/mode/1up), labial blend *finickety* in [*Munsey’s Magazine* (December 1912, p. 443)](https://archive.org/details/sim_munseys-magazine_1912-12_48_3/page/443/mode/1up), and overt *-ly* adverb *pernicketily* in [Walter de la Mare’s *Two Tales* (July 1925, p. 44)](https://archive.org/details/twotales00dela/page/44/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-208, CERT-3100-209, CERT-3100-210, CERT-3100-211, CERT-3100-213, CERT-3100-212).
- **Lorna Moon and reverse export to Scottish fiction**: Although [*SND*](https://dsl.ac.uk/entry/snd/pernicketie) cites Lorna Moon’s 1926 *Doorways in Drumorty* as a Scottish witness for *persnickety*, our Trap A audit of [*The Century Magazine*, Vol. 104, No. 3 (July 1922), p. 386](https://archive.org/details/centuryapopular06projgoog/page/n397/mode/1up) (misdated “1870” in catalog metadata) shows that Moon—a Scottish emigré screenwriter living in Hollywood—originally wrote and published “Silk Both Sides” for an American magazine audience (CERT-3100-214).

### Forty-nine years of dictionary omission, 1885–1934

Despite nationwide newspaper circulation by 1892, general dictionaries ignored sibilant *persnickety* for nearly half a century. [John Russell Bartlett’s *Dictionary of Americanisms* (4th ed., 1884 [c. 1877], p. 460)](https://archive.org/details/dictionaryofamer00bart_1/page/n513/mode/1up) entered non-sibilant *Pernickity* immediately above *Persimmon* (CERT-3100-404). Thereafter, [*The Century Dictionary* (1890, pp. 4408, 4413)](https://archive.org/details/centurydictipt1500whituoft/page/n279/mode/1up), [Funk & Wagnalls’s *Standard Dictionary* (1895, pp. 1315–1317)](https://archive.org/details/standarddictiona02funkuoft/page/n264/mode/1up), [Farmer & Henley’s *Slang and Its Analogues*, Vol. V (1902, pp. 174–175)](https://archive.org/details/slangitsanalogue05farmuoft/page/n177/mode/1up), [Joseph Wright’s *English Dialect Dictionary*, Vol. IV (1905 reissue [1903], p. 475)](https://archive.org/details/englishdialectdi04wrig/page/475/mode/1up), [Sir James Murray’s *OED1*, Vol. VII (1909 [1905 fascicle], pp. 713, 724)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.207496/page/n725/mode/1up), [*The Century Dictionary Supplement*, Vol. XII (1910 [c. 1909], pp. 972–973)](https://archive.org/details/centurydictionar0012benj/page/n230/mode/1up), and [*Webster’s New International Dictionary* (1927 impression of 1909 plates, pp. 1607, 1609—including the six-column lower pearl-type minor-entry deck)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.207990/page/n327/mode/1up) all entered *pernickety* while omitting *persnickety*, and [*Webster’s International* (1898 [1890 plates])](https://archive.org/details/webstersinternat00port/page/n1186/mode/1up), [Sylva Clapin (1902)](https://archive.org/details/newdictionaryofa00clapuoft/page/n323/mode/1up), and [Richard H. Thornton (1912)](https://archive.org/details/americanglossary02thor/page/n100/mode/1up) omitted both forms (CERT-3100-405 through CERT-3100-412, CERT-3100-314, CERT-3100-315).

## Origin mechanism and falsified hypotheses

### Two-stage morphological and phonological pathway

1. **Stage 1 — Lowland Scots *per-* + *nick / nicket* + *-ie* (`<= 1808`)**: On the pages surrounding `PERNICKITIE` in [Jamieson’s 1808 *Dictionary*, Vol. II](https://archive.org/details/etymologicaldict02jami/page/n160/mode/1up), Jamieson records `NICKET` (“A small notch”, leaf `n160`) alongside Romance-derived Scots intensive `per-` adjectives denoting fastidious precision or trimness: `PERJINK`, `PERQUER`, and `PERSHITTIE` (“Prim, precise”, leaf `n213`; CERT-3100-401). Because Northern English `FINNIKING, FINNIKY` first enters dialect lexicography seventeen years later in [John Trotter Brockett’s *Glossary of North Country Words* (1825, p. 72)](https://archive.org/details/glossaryofnorthc00brocuoft/page/n193/mode/1up)—the same year Jamieson recorded the Roxburghshire blend `PRIGNICKITIE`—*finicky* functioned as a secondary blending partner rather than the primary source of 1808 *pernicketie* (CERT-3100-402).
2. **Stage 2 — Rhotic American `-s-` epenthesis and `sn-` phonaesthetic contamination (`<= 1885`)**: Unlike non-rhotic Southern British speech (where Wright’s *EDD* recorded thirteen non-sibilant variants and zero `-s-` forms), rhotic Inland Northern and Midland American English articulated post-vocalic `/r/` directly before pre-tonic `/ˈnɪk-/`. Across the six-county Northeast Ohio settlement belt surrounding Canton—where [Table XXXI of the *Compendium of the Tenth Census* (1883, pp. 525–527)](https://archive.org/details/compendiumoftent01unitrich/page/n607/mode/1up) enumerates 31,270 Pennsylvania-born migrants alongside 2,534 Scottish-born and 7,682 Irish-born residents linked to Allegheny County, Pennsylvania (CERT-3100-413)—rhotic `/pərˈnɪkəti/` collided with the pejorative-fastidious English `sn-` cluster documented in [Wright’s *EDD*, Vol. V (1905 reissue, pp. 580–585)](https://archive.org/details/englishdialectdi05wrig/page/n591/mode/1up) (`SNICKET` / *Snickitty*, `SNIFFLTIE` / `SNIFFY`, `SNIPPET` / *Snippety*; CERT-3100-403). That `sn-` contamination simultaneously accounts for the intrusive sibilant, the 1892 two-word newspaper spellings (*per snickety*), and Louise Pound’s 1905 American gloss “Disagreeable, or snippy” (CERT-3100-208).

### Falsified folk etymologies and archival mirages

- **French *par chiquet* (“bit by bit”)**: Frequently repeated in popular columns, this derivation fails on phonology, syntax, and historical attestation. [Randle Cotgrave’s *Dictionarie of the French and English Tongues* (1611, sigs. R3r, R4v)](https://archive.org/details/cotgrave1611goog/page/n196/mode/1up) omits *chiquet* and *par chiquet* altogether (recording only adverbial *de chic à chic*, “From little to little”; CERT-3100-414), while [Émile Littré’s *Dictionnaire de la langue française*, Tome I (1873), p. 606](https://archive.org/details/dictionnairedel01litt/page/n673/mode/1up) confirms that *chiquet* is pronounced `[ʃikɛ]` with a silent final `-t`, lacks any medial `-n-`, and functions strictly in quantitative payment idioms (*payer chiquet à chiquet*, “to pay in small driblets”), never as a personal adjective meaning “fastidious” (CERT-3100-415).
- **Latin *pernicitas* / *pernicious***: Collating [Murray’s *OED1*, Vol. VII (1909, p. 713)](https://archive.org/details/newenglishdic07murruoft/page/n728/mode/1up) with [*Harpers’ Latin Dictionary* (1899 [1879 plates], p. 1349)](https://archive.org/details/harperslatindict02lewi/page/n1366/mode/1up) shows that Latin *pernicitas* meant “swiftness, fleetness, nimbleness” (yielding obsolete English *pernicity*, last attested c. 1730) and is unrelated in both consonantism and sense (CERT-3100-416).
- **Pre-1885 OCR ghosts and bound-serial misdates**: Apparent pre-1885 hits for *persnickety* dissolve on primary scan inspection into faint-type *pernicious* in the [*Daily National Intelligencer* (March 28, 1815)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83026172/1815-03-28/ed-1/?sp=2) (CERT-3100-114), line-wrapped `$1 per pack- / age` in [*The Lancaster Daily Intelligencer* (November 2, 1882)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83032300/1882-11-02/ed-1/?sp=4) (CERT-3100-115), an undated post-1892 promptbook of A. F. Nail’s [*The "Drummer Boy"*](https://archive.org/details/thedrummerboy_afnail/page/n69/mode/1up) naming Congressman Winfield Scott Kerr as attorney but cataloged as “1884” (CERT-3100-113), and bound-serial start-year misdates on [*Macmillan’s Magazine* (November 1881, tagged “1859”)](https://archive.org/details/macmillansmagaz43grovgoog/page/n88/mode/1up), [*The Atlantic Monthly* (November 1908, tagged “1857”)](https://archive.org/details/atlanticmonthly20projgoog/page/n731/mode/1up), [*The Dial* (July 16, 1914, tagged “1880”)](https://archive.org/details/dial18unkngoog/page/n61/mode/1up), and [*The Century Magazine* (July 1922, tagged “1870”)](https://archive.org/details/centuryapopular06projgoog/page/n397/mode/1up) (CERT-3100-316, CERT-3100-116, CERT-3100-215, CERT-3100-214).
