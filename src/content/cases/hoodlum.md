## 1. Prior Scholarship and the September 2026 *OED3* Revision

For more than a century after Henry Bradley drafted the [first-edition *Oxford English Dictionary* entry for *hoodlum*](https://archive.org/details/oxforddictionaryv5p1unse) (H fascicle, 1899; Vol. V, 1901, p. 371, `CERT-1000-040`), standard reference works treated the word as an unsolved Pacific Coast mystery. Opening with the *Sacramento Weekly Union* of 24 February 1872, Bradley remarked that its origin in San Francisco was already lost by 1877 amid *"many fictitious stories, concocted mainly by newspaper humourists."* Mitford M. Mathews's *Dictionary of Americanisms* (1951) and [*OED2* (1989)](https://archive.org/details/OXD1989ENEN) later moved the earliest general citation back five months to 6 September 1871 (*Cincinnati Commercial Supplement*), [*Green's Dictionary of Slang*](https://greensdictofslang.com/entry/dohqtvy) added an August 1871 [*Overland Monthly*](https://archive.org/details/overlandmonthly7176sanfrich) witness (`CERT-1000-041`), and J. E. Lighter's *Historical Dictionary of American Slang* (1997) noted a 16 February 1868 *Golden Era* reference to *"the Hoodlum Gang."*

Calibrating this dossier requires explicitly crediting the researchers who broke the 1868 barrier as well as the scholars of the German derivation:

* **Fred Shapiro, the [Frederick Bee Project](https://www.frederickbee.com/hoodlum.html), and *OED3* (September 2026):** Following a December 2015 thread on [English StackExchange Q291676](https://english.stackexchange.com/questions/291676/what-is-the-lost-origin-of-hoodlum), researcher "Tony" of the [Frederick Bee Project](https://www.frederickbee.com/hoodlum.html) published on 3 February 2016 the December 1866 San Francisco *Daily Evening Bulletin* and *Daily Alta California* Police Court reports on the juvenile *"Hoodlum Band"* and their second-hand clothes fence Lazarus Moses, crediting **Fred Shapiro** for providing the [14 December 1866 *Evening Bulletin* microfilm PDF](https://www.frederickbee.com/hoodlum.pdf) (also noted by Stephen Goranson on [OUPblog, 21 December 2016](https://blog.oup.com/2016/12/loom-word-origin/)). In **September 2026**, [*OED3*](https://www.oed.com/dictionary/hoodlum_n) formally revised *hoodlum, n.* (alongside [*hoodlumism, n.*](https://www.oed.com/dictionary/hoodlumism_n) and *hoodlum wagon, n.*), adopting the 14 December 1866 *Evening Bulletin* quotation (`CERT-1000-001`), summarizing Lazarus Moses's arrest as owner of a second-hand clothes shop, and noting tentatively that the word *"may be < German regional (Bavaria) Hudellump, Hudellumpe, Hudelump, (now usually) Hodalump rag-collector, ragamuffin, scoundrel."* Even so, *OED3* retains *"Of uncertain origin"* (*"The origin of the name of this gang and its members is unclear"*), jumps eleven years in Sense 1 from 14 December 1866 to August 1877 (*Boston Journal*), and records no attributive compound before *hoodlum wagon* (1882).
* **C. G. (1888), Peter Tamony (1969), and Gerald Cohen:** San Francisco folklorist **Peter Tamony** championed Bavarian *hodalum / huddellump* (following George Gessner and John T. Krumpelmann) while refuting 1877 press fables in ["Hoodlums and Folk Etymology" (*Western Folklore* 28.1, January 1969, pp. 44–48)](https://files.shsmo.org/manuscripts/columbia/C3939.pdf) (`CERT-1000-050`), followed by Robert K. Barnhart (1988) and Anatoly Liberman ([OUPblog](https://blog.oup.com/2016/02/etymology-idioms-hooligan-ok-love/), 2008). Primary-scan audit uncovers an overlooked precursor eighty-one years before Tamony: in [*American Notes and Queries* (16 June 1888, p. 83)](https://archive.org/details/americannotesque01philuoft) (`CERT-1000-043`), contributor **"C. G."** linked *hoodlum* to German *hudeln* ('to loaf'), *hudler*, and *hudel* ('trash, a vagabond'), comparing *bummer* from *Bummler*. Auditing **Gerald Cohen**'s [August 2026 Research CV](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.mst.edu/dist/8/416/files/2026/08/RESEARCH-CV-August-2026.pdf) confirms that while Cohen's urban-prosopography method informs our approach, neither *Comments on Etymology* nor *Studies in Slang* has previously treated *hoodlum*.

## 2. Primary Chronology: December 1866 to the Spring 1871 Semantic Generalization

After San Francisco police [arrested five juveniles on 13 December 1866](https://www.frederickbee.com/boysarrest.pdf)—William Sullivan, William Frattiger, Christopher J. Conley, John Hamilton, and John Hudson—for burglarizing the Chicago, American Exchange, and International Hotels, and Officer Frederick O. Fuller [arrested thirteen-year-old Tommy Lloyd on 14 December](https://www.frederickbee.com/lloyd.pdf) at Third and Jessie Streets, the *Daily Evening Bulletin* (`CERT-1000-001`, `CERT-1000-002`, `CERT-1000-004`, `CERT-1000-006`) and *Daily Alta California* (`CERT-1000-003`) tracked the case. On [14 December 1866](https://www.frederickbee.com/hoodlum.pdf) (`CERT-1000-001`), the *Bulletin* already uses the gang title alongside quoted singular and plural count nouns:

> It is doubtful if any thieves ever did a more driving business than these young fellows, for all of their plunder has been stolen since last Saturday night. They call themselves the "Hoodlum Band," and unless their thieving propensities are nipped in the bud, the reputation of Orliniski will fade before that of a "Hoodlum." The worst feature of the case is that the boys were encouraged to steal by an old "Fagan," who purchased whatever they brought him, and promised them that the police should never get any information from his place. This man will be prosecuted immediately. Among other exploits of the "Hoodlums" was the stealing of a lot of keys from the pockets of a clerk in an office on Montgomery street, to enter the office and unlock and rob the safe of $2 in silver.

Seven days later, Carson City's [*The Carson Daily Appeal* (21 December 1866, p. 2, col. 2)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn84022040/1866-12-21/ed-1/?sp=2) (`CERT-1000-005`) furnishes the earliest verified out-of-state witness and the earliest full-sheet Library of Congress masthead corroboration:

> BIG THIEVES PESTERED BY LITTLE ONES.—The San Francisco police has lately discovered the existence of a gang of boy-thieves in that city who style themselves the "Hoodlum Band" who have been doing a wholesale business in the pilfering way. This fact having leaked out, the big thieves who "destroy widows houses" and grind the faces of the poor, have set up a terrible wail, and the boys are to be prosecuted in accordance with their devilish proficiencies. That virtuous dragon, the Bulletin, has lost no time in consigning the youthful scamps to the fathomless depths of its awful damnation.

In San Francisco, fence Lazarus Moses was arrested on [15 December 1866](https://www.frederickbee.com/faginarticle.png) (`CERT-1000-002`), convicted in five minutes on [20 December](https://www.frederickbee.com/faginverdict.pdf) (`CERT-1000-004`), and fined $300 by Judge Alfred Rix on [27 December](https://www.frederickbee.com/faginfine.pdf) (`CERT-1000-006`).

Five Chronicling America scans from April–July 1871 bridge *OED3*'s eleven-year Sense 1 gap (1866–1877), capturing the spring 1871 shift from one crew's moniker to an unquoted plural class noun nearly five months before *OED2*'s 6 September 1871 baseline. First, [*Gold Hill Daily News* of 15 April 1871 (p. 2, col. 3)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn84022046/1871-04-15/ed-1/?sp=2) (`CERT-1000-007`) uses unquoted *the Hoodlums* in describing ruffians at a San Francisco Swiss Guard Sunday outing:

> Had there been a Bill Tell amongst the Swiss Guard on Sunday, this would have been done; but unfortunately, that patriot's spirit did not animate any of his countrymen, and in consequence the Hoodlums had it pretty nearly all their own way. For the next few months the heading, "Disgraceful Row at a Picnic," will be a regular feature of Monday's newspaper; and as Police Judge Sawyer has Congressional aspirations, and now makes a friend of every culprit brought before him by inflicting only nominal penalties, it is useless to expect that he will do anything to abate them.

Second, [*Gold Hill Daily News* of 17 April 1871 (p. 2, col. 2)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn84022046/1871-04-17/ed-1/?sp=2) (`CERT-1000-008`) prints a Western Union wire dispatch from San Francisco demonstrating open-class count syntax (*a gang of Hoodlums*):

> SAN FRANCISCO, April 17—1 P. M.—At the Shields Guard excursion to Petaluma yesterday, a gang of Hoodlums, who had accompanied the excursionists, commenced a row by smashing crockery at the restaurants, etc. The Guard interfered, and Mike Thompson, one of the roughs, stabbed John P. Yates, of the Guard, twice in the breast, inflicting slight wounds. He was arrested and held in $1,000 bail.

Third, [*Gold Hill Daily News* of 1 May 1871 (p. 2, col. 2)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn84022046/1871-05-01/ed-1/?sp=2) (`CERT-1000-009`) attests productive attributive compounding eleven years ahead of *OED3*'s *hoodlum wagon* (1882):

> A NEAT THING IN NECK-TIES.—Neck-tie festivals being now all the rage, the Butler (Kansas) vigilantes, not to be behind the fashion, held one a few days since. The neck-tie used was made of hemp, and the young man who wore it will need no more neck-ties. This should be the "Hoodlum neck-tie."

Fourth, Carson City's [*The Daily State Register* of 18 May 1871 (p. 2, col. 1)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn84020345/1871-05-18/ed-1/?sp=2) (`CERT-1000-010`) prints a standalone brieflet on municipal chain-gang enforcement:

> GOOD!—Some of the Hoodlums of San Francisco have quit annoying decent people, and gone quietly to work in the chain-gang.

Fifth, Sonora's [*The Union Democrat* of 1 July 1871 (p. 1, col. 5)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85025134/1871-07-01/ed-1/?sp=1) (`CERT-1000-011`) reports San Francisco Police Chief Patrick Crowley detailing four officers to bar rowdies from a Martinez excursion (*'No "Hoodlums" were allowed on board'*).

## 3. Prosopography of Lazarus Moses's Network and Origin Amnesia

Cross-linking seven editions of *Langley's San Francisco Directory* ([1864](https://archive.org/details/sanfranciscodire1864lang)–[1872](https://archive.org/details/sanfranciscodire1872langrich)), the [1866 San Francisco Great Register](https://archive.org/details/californiagreatr1866unse), census rolls, and the [*San Francisco Municipal Reports for FY 1866–67*](https://archive.org/details/sanfranciscomuni66sanfrich) reconstructs the Barbary Coast micro-network behind the December 1866 arrests:

| [23 Jul 1866 Great Register](https://archive.org/details/californiagreatr1866unse) | Line 5142: `Moses, Lazarus \| 40 \| Prussia \| Merchant \| 822 Montgomery` | Naturalized 7 Nov 1864 (U.S. Dist. Ct., SF); corrects [1946 DAR](https://archive.org/details/sanfranciscogrea00daug) typos `Hoses` & `324` |
| [1867](https://archive.org/details/sanfranciscodire1867lang)–[1868 Directories](https://archive.org/details/sanfranciscodire1868lang) | Raphael Levy (`463 Jessie` junk; `502 Pacific` clothier); Chicago Hotel (`214–222 Pacific`) | Caroline's ex-husband Levy, partner Charles Wehring, and burglarized hoteliers Heimburg, Droste & Pfefferle are all German |
| [FY 1866–67 Municipal Reports](https://archive.org/details/sanfranciscomuni66sanfrich) | Pp. 166, 298, 303–304: 3 fence arrests citywide; 5 boys committed Dec 1866 | Boys enter an 82% U.S.-born Industrial School cohort, generalizing the gang name in English |

This institutional bottleneck explains why San Franciscans forgot the word's origin within eight years. Five arrested boys (Anglo-Irish surnames Sullivan, Conley, Hamilton, Hudson, and Lloyd plus Swiss-surnamed William Frattiger) entered an 82 percent U.S.-born Industrial School cohort in December 1866. Four years of English-language youth-gang incubation severed the label from Moses's shop: by 1874–1876, both B. E. Lloyd ([*Lights and Shades in San Francisco*, 1876, p. 297](https://archive.org/details/lightsshadesinsa00lloy), `CERT-1000-012`) and Walter M. Fisher ([*The Californians*, 1876, pp. 79–80](https://archive.org/details/californians00fish), `CERT-1000-042`) called its derivation unknown, and across fifty-eight Chronicling America German-press hits—including the *San Francisco Abend-Post* excerpt in [*Washingtoner Journal* on 1 June 1874 (p. 4, col. 2)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn84026744/1874-06-01/ed-1/?sp=4) (`CERT-1000-033`)—typesetters set *Hoodlums* in Antiqua type inside Fraktur prose as an English loanword.

## 4. German Underworld and Dialect Hypothesis vs. Attested Prosopography

Pre-1871 German underworld (*Gaunersprache*) and regional lexicons match both sides of Moses's operation while requiring three bibliographic corrections:

* **Underworld *Haderlumpsammler* and Dual-Sense *Hudelump*:** F. C. B. Avé-Lallemant ([*Das deutsche Gaunerthum*, 1862, Th. III, p. 145](https://archive.org/details/dasdeutschegaun01lalgoog/page/n563/mode/2up), `CERT-1000-029`) documents rag-and-old-clothes collecting (*Haderlumpsammler*) as a classic criminal cover trade (*Scheingeschäft*) for fences, whereas A. F. Thiele ([*Die jüdischen Gauner in Deutschland*, 1842](https://sammlungen.ub.uni-frankfurt.de/freimann/content/titleinfo/774075), `CERT-1000-030`) rules out Hebrew-stem *Kochemer-Loschen* cant. Crucially, Johann Fischart's [*Geschichtklitterung* (1590; ed. Scheible, *Das Kloster*, Bd. 8, 1847, pp. 160, 234)](https://archive.org/details/johannfischarts00murngoog/page/n249/mode/2up) (`CERT-1000-021`) attests single-*l* *Hudelump / Hudelumpe* for both the sack-carrying rag-collector bogeyman (*"den Hudelump, vnd den Mann mit dem sack"*) and prowling street-rogue youths (*"Spatzieren umb das Hauß, Hudelumpe"*), alongside Stieler's *Hudellumpen* and *Hudelvolk* ([1691, col. 804](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_N8haAAAAcAAJ/page/n465/mode/2up), `CERT-1000-020`), Stalder's Swiss *Hudel* and *hudeln* 'dishonest in trade' ([1812, II: 59](https://archive.org/details/10584420bsb/page/n74/mode/2up), `CERT-1000-024`), Vilmar's Hessian *Hudel ~ Hoddel* ([1868, p. 177](https://archive.org/details/idiotikonvonkurh00vilmuoft/page/n191/mode/2up), `CERT-1000-025`), Kehrein's *Lumpes / Hurlump* ([1862](https://archive.org/details/volksspracheund00unkngoog/page/n568/mode/2up), `CERT-1000-026`), Wander's *Hudelmann* ([1870, col. 796](https://archive.org/details/deutschessprich00sprigoog/page/n421/mode/2up), `CERT-1000-027`), and U.S. Fraktur newsprint *Hudel* ([*Der liberale Beobachter*, 7 May 1850](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn87052123/1850-05-07/ed-1/?sp=1), `CERT-1000-031`) and *Haderlump* ([*Westliche Blätter*, 10 Nov 1867](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045582/1867-11-10/ed-1/?sp=3), `CERT-1000-032`).
* **Phonology, Ghost Citations, and Hypothesis Boundary:** J. A. Schmeller ([*Bayerisches Wörterbuch*, 1828, II: 468](https://archive.org/details/10385917bsb/page/n160/mode/2up), `CERT-1000-022`) transcribes Bavarian post-nasal assimilation as *(Lumpm) / (Hädo'lumpm)*; in English juveniles' mouths, `['hʊdəlˌlʊmp]` yields `/ˈhʊdləm/` via medial syncope and unstressed *-mp -> -m* reduction. Primary scans also eliminate Barnhart's unattested geminate **Huddellump* (absent in Schmeller), [Merriam-Webster's](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/hoodlum) Swabian *hudelum* (absent in Schmid's [1831 *Schwäbisches Wörterbuch*](https://archive.org/details/11281136bsb/page/n310/mode/2up), `CERT-1000-023`; first recorded in Fischer 1911), and Internet Archive's misdated `1854` Grimm [*Deutsches Wörterbuch* Bd. 4.2](https://archive.org/details/deutscheswrter42grimuoft/page/n943/mode/2up) (true imprint 1877, `CERT-1000-028`). Because no contemporary witness recorded the spoken moment of coinage at 822–824 Montgomery Street, this Rotwelsch/*Hudelump* contact pathway remains a linguistic hypothesis clearly separated from the attested court prosopography.

## 5. The August 1877 *New York Sun* Hoax and Rejected Leads

Following the July 1877 San Francisco anti-Chinese riots, three contradictory folk etymologies erupted within sixty days. Where Bartlett ([1877, pp. 293, 785](https://archive.org/details/dictionaryameri01bartgoog), `CERT-1000-044`) and *OED1* (`CERT-1000-040`) traced the *"Muldoon -> noodlums"* tale to the 26 September 1877 *Congregationalist*, Morgantown's [*The New Dominion* of 8 September 1877 (p. 1, col. 4)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn86092182/1877-09-08/ed-1/?sp=1) (`CERT-1000-013`; cf. [*Deseret Evening News*, 23 August 1877](https://newspapers.lib.utah.edu/details?id=23167516)) reprints the verbatim paragraph eighteen days earlier credited to the *New York Sun*:

> NEW YORK Sun: A slang word in common use, "hoodlum," was maltreated in its birth in this way: A newspaper man in San Francisco, in attempting to coin a word to designate a gang of street Arabs under the beck of one Muldoon, hit on the idea of dubbing them the noodlums; that is, simply reversing the leader's name. In writing the word the strokes of the n did not correspond in height, and the compositor took it for hoodlum. Hoodlum it is, and probably ever will be.

By 30 September 1877, [*The Daily Astorian* (p. 2, col. 1)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn96061149/1877-09-30/ed-1/?sp=2) (`CERT-1000-014`) had mutated Muldoon into an essayist signing *"Noodlum,"* and [*The New Orleans Daily Democrat* (30 October 1879, p. 1)](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83026413/1879-10-30/ed-1/?sp=1) (`CERT-1000-016`) juxtaposed all three 1877 fables (*Muldoon*, *"Huddle 'em!"*, and Zouave *"hoods"*). [*Langley's 1867 Directory* (p. 360)](https://archive.org/details/sanfranciscodire1867lang) lists only four adult Muldoons in San Francisco, none linked to the 1866 gang.
