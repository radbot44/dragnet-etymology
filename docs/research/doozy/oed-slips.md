# Dictionary correspondence notes (not submitted)

- **doozy, adjective, 1903 (existing baseline; transcription correction).** Al Kleberg, *Slang Fables from Afar* (Baltimore: Phoenix Publishing Co., 1903), p. 83: “he began to evolve Schemes—one Doozy scheme followed the other”. [Printed page](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=umn.31951001632845h&seq=87) and [copyright leaf](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=umn.31951001632845h&seq=6). Credit Dave Wilton’s prior identification. No earlier date is claimed.
- **doozy, adjective, 1911 (narrower gloss).** Louise Pound, “A Second Word-List from Nebraska,” *Dialect Notes* III, part VII, p. 543: “doozy, adj. ‘Sporty,’ or ‘flossy.’” [Printed page](https://archive.org/details/dialectnotes03newhuoft/page/543/mode/1up) and [issue opening](https://archive.org/details/dialectnotes03newhuoft/page/497/mode/1up).
- **doozy, adjective, 1913 (fascicle date).** Elsie Warnock, “Terms of Approbation and Eulogy in American Dialect Speech,” *Dialect Notes* IV, part I, p. 21: “Where’s that doozy fellow I saw you with?” and “She’s a trifle bit doozy.” [Printed page](https://archive.org/details/dialectnotes04newhuoft/page/21/mode/1up) and [1913 fascicle](https://archive.org/details/dialectnotes04newhuoft/page/1/mode/1up). Warnock cites Pound. This is a date correction where the bound-volume year 1917 is used, not an antedating of the word.
- **dozy, noun, 1916 (existing baseline; part-of-speech note).** Louise Pound, “Word-List from Nebraska (III),” *Dialect Notes* IV, part IV, p. 274: “Isn’t that fish a dozy?” [Printed page](https://archive.org/details/dialectnotes04newhuoft/page/274/mode/1up) and [1916 fascicle](https://archive.org/details/dialectnotes04newhuoft/page/263/mode/1up). The surrounding entry prints *adj.*, but the quotation is nominal.

Do not present the 1901 rhyme as a same-sense antedating or the reported Akeley letter as a dated manuscript. Check live dictionary treatments before writing to editors.
