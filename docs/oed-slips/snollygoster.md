## Draft Note 3 — snollygoster, n. (1855–1866 Partisan Lodge Context, Satire, and Credited Candidate Insult)
- **Box 1 (Word or phrase)**: snollygoster, n. (*Snollygosters* in partisan lodge title; *Snolly-goster* as personal candidate insult)
- **Box 2 (Year of quotation)**: 1855 / 1866
- **Box 3 (Full text of quotation)**: [1855 lodge context:] The Immortal Snollygosters. This is the name of a new order lately organized in Kentucky, at ‘the head and front’ of which is Major Beatty, of Bath county, which promises to supersede the dark lantern sink holes. [1866 candidate insult, previously quoted by J. Lighter 2024 & Merriam-Webster:] The Democrats of Leavenworth have nominated a full County ticket. We see that “R. Cole Foster, the Snolly-goster,” is one of the nominees for the State Senate. He had better stay at home; there will be no person at the Capital, next Winter, to properly take care of his morals.
- **Box 4 (Bibliographical details)**: *The Southern Banner* (Athens, Georgia), Vol. XXIV, No. 16, 28 June 1855, p. 3, col. 4 (via Lexington *Kentucky Statesman*; cf. J. B. Murphy, *Snolly-goster Ebenezer*, St. Louis, 1864, pp. 3, 5); *White Cloud Kansas Chief* (White Cloud, Kansas), Vol. X, No. 17, 25 October 1866, p. 2, col. 2 (credited to J. Lighter, ADS-L, 6 Jan. 2024, and Merriam-Webster).
- **Box 5 (Any other information)**: Documents 1855–1866 political context and candidate invective prior to H. W. J. Ham's 1892–1895 office-seeker definition (`CERT-3200-201`, `CERT-3200-205`, `CERT-3200-206`). Primary Scans: https://gahistoricnewspapers.galileo.usg.edu/lccn/sn82014069/1855-06-28/ed-1/seq-3/ and https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn82015486/1866-10-25/ed-1/?sp=2

## Draft Slip 4 — snollygoster, n. (27 October 1892 — Pre-1895 Witness to H. W. J. Ham's Office-Seeking Definition)
- **Box 1 (Word or phrase)**: snollygoster, n. ('a person with an unquenchable thirst for political office')
- **Box 2 (Year of quotation)**: 1892
- **Box 3 (Full text of quotation)**: “SNOLLYGOSTER” is a word coined by Ham, the orator from the Georgia mountains. A snollygoster he defined as a “fellow with an unquenchable thirst for office with neither the power to get it nor capacity to fill it.” There are several specimens of the “snollygoster” type among the Third party in Vance connty [sic].
- **Box 4 (Bibliographical details)**: *The Gold Leaf* (Henderson, North Carolina), Vol. XI, No. 45, Thursday, 27 October 1892, p. 2, col. 3 (following campaign reports in *The Sun*, New York, 29 Sept. 1892, p. 6, col. 3, and *The Morning News*, Savannah, 4 Oct. 1892, p. 4, col. 1; cf. *Iowa State Bystander*, Des Moines, 15 Nov. 1895, p. 2, col. 1, identifying 'Col. Hamm, editor of the Georgia Cracker').
- **Box 5 (Any other information)**: Clearer pre-1895 lexical definition for the office-seeking sense (`CERT-3200-211`, `CERT-3200-213`); hold pending a separate check for Ham's upstream autumn 1892 Georgia printing. On etymology, dialectal *goster* ('boisterous bragging') is a plausible ingredient, and the 1845–1909 chronology weakens deriving *snollygoster* from 1909 Maryland *snallygaster* without proving the reverse. Primary Scan: https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn91068402/1892-10-27/ed-1/?sp=2
